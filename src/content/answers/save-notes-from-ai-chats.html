How to keep the good parts of your AI conversations

Short answer: take the good parts out of the chat as you go — star them, copy them into your notes, or export the conversation to Markdown. Finding them later, buried in a conversation named after a throwaway first question, rarely works.

Most of what an assistant produces is disposable. A small fraction is genuinely worth keeping — and that fraction is the hardest to find again later, because it is buried in a conversation named after a throwaway opening question.

Why "I'll find it later" fails

Three things happen between now and later. The conversation's title stops describing what it became. The platform's search only reliably covers titles and recent content. And you start using a second assistant, at which point there is no single place that knows about everything you have asked.

So the answer you want is somewhere across hundreds of chats on two or three services, under a title that describes something else.

Four approaches, in increasing order of effort

  1. Ask for a keepable summary before you leave. "Give me the final version as a single self-contained block, no preamble." One message you can find, instead of a conclusion spread over forty.
  2. Rename the conversation when you finish. Thirty seconds, and it converts an unsearchable list into an index. More on this.
  3. Copy the keepers out immediately. Into whatever you already use — notes, a document, a ticket. The discipline is doing it at the moment you recognise the value, not later.
  4. Keep a full local archive. Removes the prediction entirely: you no longer have to decide now what will matter later.

What goes wrong with copy-paste

Copying out of a chat is lossier than it looks. Rendered maths comes out as a mangled duplicate of every symbol, or vanishes completely, depending on which renderer the site uses. Code blocks lose their language and sometimes their indentation. Diagrams copy as a run-together string of their own labels. Images become nothing at all.

If you are keeping technical answers, check what you actually pasted rather than assuming. The failure is silent and you find it months later.
