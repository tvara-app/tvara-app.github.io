A toolkit for long AI conversations

Long conversations fail in five distinct ways. Most advice treats them as one problem, which is why most advice is "start a new chat".

1 · The page gets slow

Hundreds of messages is tens of thousands of page elements, all of which the browser keeps measured. Typing lags, scrolling stutters, and streaming answers get worse the longer the chat gets.

Without tools: reload the tab, close other tabs, avoid pasting large files inline. With Tvara: off-screen messages stop costing what on-screen ones do, with nothing deleted. The full explanation.

2 · You cannot navigate it

A four-hundred-message conversation has no table of contents. Finding the part where you settled on an approach means scrolling and guessing.

Without tools: keep a separate notes file with your own anchors. With Tvara: a minimap of the whole conversation on the right edge, an outline of what was discussed, and jump-to-message. Free on every supported site.

3 · You cannot search it

Ctrl+F only searches what is rendered, and long chats unload their own history. Platform search is title-first and does not cross platforms.

Without tools: scroll to the top to force older messages to mount, then search. With Tvara: search that reaches unloaded messages, and archive search across every platform at once. More on this.

4 · You lose the context when you start over

The standard fix for a slow chat is a new chat, and the cost is everything the model knew. You end up pasting fragments of the old conversation into the new one from memory.

Without tools: keep a running summary you can paste. With Tvara: pull a specific past answer straight into the prompt you are writing, from the archive, without hunting for it.

5 · You run out of allowance without warning

Limits are rolling and mostly unpublished, so the cut-off arrives mid-answer.

Without tools: learn your window and front-load expensive work. With Tvara: the figures platforms already publish to their own interfaces, in one panel, with resets — and "not reported" where a provider genuinely publishes nothing. More on this.

The sixth problem, which is not about length

Your history is on someone else's server. Conversations get deleted by retention policies, shared accounts and mis-clicks, and account exports are slow snapshots that arrive by email. A local archive makes the other five problems easier and this one disappear. Exports · Deletion
